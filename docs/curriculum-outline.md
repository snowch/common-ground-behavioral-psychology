# Curriculum and evidence map

## 1. Describe the action before explaining it

Operationally define a behaviour and distinguish a baseline from an explanation.

- A useful description can be checked.
- Record the starting pattern.
- The observer is part of the method.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: What is learning](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-1-what-is-learning); [Staddon, Cerutti (2003): Operant Conditioning](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.54.101601.145124)

## 2. A cue can predict what comes next

Identify the conditioned stimulus and response without confusing prediction with operant consequences.

- Learning can concern predictive relations.
- Name the cue and the learned response.
- Prediction matters more than coincidence.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Classical conditioning](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-2-classical-conditioning); [Rescorla (1988): Pavlovian conditioning: It's not what you think it is.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0003-066X.43.3.151)

## 3. Consequences can change an action

Classify reinforcement and punishment by their measured effects, distinguishing addition from removal.

- Start with the action and its consequence.
- Positive and negative mean addition and removal.
- Removal can also reduce behaviour.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Operant conditioning](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-3-operant-conditioning); [Staddon, Cerutti (2003): Operant Conditioning](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.54.101601.145124)

## 4. Small steps can build a skill

Design successive approximations and distinguish skill acquisition from an access problem.

- Shape the behaviour that matters.
- Break complex actions into teachable parts.
- Distinguish cannot from cannot here.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Operant conditioning](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-3-operant-conditioning); [Staddon, Cerutti (2003): Operant Conditioning](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.54.101601.145124)

## 5. The pattern of consequences matters

Distinguish ratio and interval schedules while avoiding deterministic predictions about individuals.

- Schedules describe arranged relations.
- Ratio and interval concern different requirements.
- Delay changes the learning problem.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Operant conditioning](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-3-operant-conditioning); [Staddon, Cerutti (2003): Operant Conditioning](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.54.101601.145124)

## 6. A learned response can return

Explain extinction as new learning and distinguish response return from erasure failure.

- Extinction changes the predictive relation.
- Return has different forms.
- Plan for changing conditions.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Classical conditioning](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-2-classical-conditioning); [Bouton (1993): Context, time, and memory retrieval in the interference paradigms of Pavlovian learning.](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.114.1.80)

## 7. A cue is not the whole situation

Distinguish generalization from discrimination and identify the conditions controlling an action.

- Learning can extend beyond a trained cue.
- Discrimination depends on a relation.
- Design transfer deliberately.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Classical conditioning](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-2-classical-conditioning); [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Operant conditioning](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-3-operant-conditioning)

## 8. Watching can teach more than imitation

Distinguish learning from a model, immediate imitation and later performance.

- Learning can use another person’s actions.
- Attention and practice still matter.
- Observed consequences shape expectations.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Observational learning](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-4-observational-learning-modeling); [Bandura, Ross, Ross (1961): Transmission of aggression through imitation of aggressive models.](https://doi.org/10.1037/h0045925)

## 9. Habit depends on repeated contexts

Distinguish habit from intention and design a low-stakes change with a meaningful outcome.

- Repeated contexts can cue an action.
- Change the conditions, not just the slogan.
- Measure the outcome and its limits.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: Operant conditioning](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-3-operant-conditioning); [Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts (2009): How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world](https://doi.org/10.1002/ejsp.674)

## 10. Change should be useful and freely chosen

Design an ethical behavioural evaluation that tests a meaningful outcome and preserves agency.

- A function is a hypothesis to test.
- A change needs an evaluation design.
- Preserve agency in the application.
- What does the evidence allow?

Sources: [OpenStax Psychology 2e: What is learning](https://openstax.org/books/psychology-2e/pages/6-1-what-is-learning); [Staddon, Cerutti (2003): Operant Conditioning](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev.psych.54.101601.145124)
